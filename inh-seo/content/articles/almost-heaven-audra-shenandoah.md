<p>Almost Heaven's Audra runs <strong>$7,575</strong> and the Shenandoah <strong>$10,650</strong>. Both are cedar barrel saunas, both ship with a Harvia heater, and both carry a warranty that splits in a way most buyers never see. The sauna room is covered by a Limited Lifetime Warranty. The heating elements inside it are covered for <strong>one year</strong>.</p>

<p>We do not sell Almost Heaven. We sell 17 barrel saunas from other manufacturers, so we have no reason to talk you out of this one. What follows is what their own published documents say, and what we would put beside each unit if you asked us.</p>

<h2>The warranty split, in their own words</h2>

<p>Almost Heaven's <a href="https://almostheaven.com/pages/warranty" rel="nofollow">warranty page</a> states it plainly. Each sauna comes with a Limited Lifetime Warranty against manufacturing defects on all components. Then, four sentences later: your Harvia heater comes with a 1-year warranty on the heating elements (coils) and a 5-year warranty on the other heating components.</p>

<p>Lifetime on the room. One year on the coils. Same page.</p>

<p>The coils are the part that fails. Everything else in a barrel sauna is cedar, hardware and glass, and cedar outdoors is a maintenance question rather than a warranty question. The element is the consumable, and it is the shortest term on the page.</p>

<p>Three other things on that page are worth knowing before you buy. The warranty <strong>does not transfer to a second-hand buyer</strong>, so resale value carries no coverage with it. It excludes normal weathering and wear, improper assembly, and weather events, which for an outdoor barrel is a meaningful set of exclusions. And it does not cover electrician costs incurred from a warranty claim, so if a covered part needs an electrician to swap it, that is your bill.</p>

<h2>This is a pattern, not a quirk</h2>

<p>We researched heater warranty terms across the brands we actually stock, and the same split appears every time.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>HUUM</strong> offers a 5-year limited warranty with the heating elements <strong>excluded outright</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Narvi</strong> covers the stove for 2 years and the resistors for 12 months.</li>
<li><strong>Harvia</strong> covers heaters for 2 years, with an extra year if you register the unit. Dealers state the element terms inconsistently.</li>
</ul>

<p>Almost Heaven settles that last one. <a href="https://harviagroup.com/harvia-strengthens-its-position-in-the-united-states-by-acquiring-the-business-of-almost-heaven-saunas-llc/" rel="nofollow">Harvia acquired Almost Heaven in December 2018</a>, so the terms on the Almost Heaven warranty page are Harvia publishing its own numbers rather than a dealer's recollection of them. One year on coils, five on the rest of the heater.</p>

<p>Three manufacturers, three different warranty documents, one shared shape: <strong>the part most likely to fail carries the shortest term</strong>. Terms vary by dealer even within a brand, so confirm yours in writing before you pay.</p>

<h2>The Audra at $7,575</h2>

<p>A 2-4 person canopy barrel. The canopy is a covered porch section at the front, which buys you a dry place to stand while you open the door in February and costs you interior length for the same overall footprint. If the sauna lives at the end of a garden path in a wet climate, that is a reasonable trade. If it sits three steps from a back door, you are paying for shelter you already have.</p>

<p>Almost Heaven also lists a Quick Ship Audra at <strong>$8,973</strong>, which is $1,398 more for faster delivery on the same unit.</p>

<p><strong>What we would put beside it.</strong> The <a href="/collections/barrel-saunas">Dundalk LeisureCraft Granby</a> is $7,086 and seats 2-3 in Canadian red cedar with no canopy. The LeisureCraft Timber Harmony is $6,783 and seats 4. If the canopy is not doing real work in your climate, both put more interior in the same money.</p>

<h2>The Shenandoah at $10,650</h2>

<p>A 4-person barrel, and the closest thing in our range is close enough to be useful: the <strong>Scandia 4-Person Electric Barrel Sauna Kit at $10,800</strong>. Same capacity, same form factor, $150 apart, built in the USA in cedar.</p>

<p>At that price the decision stops being about the sauna and starts being about the heater and the terms behind it. Ask both sellers the same three questions. What is the element covered for, in writing. Does the warranty transfer if you sell the house. Who pays the electrician on a covered claim.</p>

<p>Our <a href="/collections/outdoor-saunas">outdoor saunas</a> ship free to the lower 48 and stop at the curb. Getting the crate inside is $600. Inside and assembled is $1,800, which covers everything except electrical work, because the wiring has to meet your local code and pass local inspection. We publish those numbers because they are the ones that surprise people.</p>

<h2>What their listings do not tell you</h2>

<p>We read all 57 sauna cabins Almost Heaven publishes. <strong>Not one product listing states a warranty term.</strong> The Limited Lifetime Warranty and the 1-year coil term both live on a separate page you have to go looking for, and 31 of the 57 name a Harvia heater without saying what covers it.</p>

<p>That is not unusual and it is not dishonest. It is the industry norm, and it is why the warranty question has to be yours to ask rather than theirs to volunteer. Their four infrared cabins publish no wavelength either, which puts them exactly where the rest of the category sits.</p>

<h2>Where the two ranges sit against each other</h2>

<p>Almost Heaven's 57 cabins start between <strong>$4,037 and $27,815, median $7,806</strong>. Our 17 barrel saunas run <strong>$4,999 to $16,980, median $7,940</strong>. Both figures are entry prices, the cheapest way into each unit, so they compare like for like.</p>

<p>The medians are <strong>$134 apart</strong>. That is the useful finding here. Their entry point is about $960 below ours and their ceiling is higher for a barrel, but across the middle of both catalogues the pricing is effectively the same, and the Audra and the Shenandoah sit right in that middle. When two ranges cost the same, price stops being the deciding factor and wood, heater and warranty terms start being it.</p>

<h2>Who should buy Almost Heaven</h2>

<p>Someone who wants a US-built barrel from a company that has been doing it since 1978, is comfortable with a lifetime room warranty and a one-year element, and either does not plan to sell the house or does not care that the coverage stops when they do. That is a defensible purchase and we would not argue with it.</p>

<p>Someone who wants the longest element coverage they can get should ask every seller for the element term in writing, ours included. It is a short question and it is the one that separates a $10,000 sauna that lasts from one that needs a $600 part in year three.</p>
