# Chromotherapy — citation pass proposal

**The article is careful about its own subject and careless about the thing it compares against.**
Every claim it makes about chromotherapy is hedged, sourced and limited, and its "What We Still
Don't Know" section is better than most pages in the estate. The defects are all in the contrast
term: sauna heat, cited to a paper that says the evidence is not well established.

**So none of this changes the article's recommendation.** A buyer reading it before and after gets
the same advice about chromotherapy.

## B. The two that are worse than misattribution

**CONTRADICTED.** The decision ruleset reads:

> If your budget is tight and you're interested only in core sauna benefits, then choose a model
> without chromotherapy to save money — **Evidence: High for heat benefits (Laukkanen et al., 2018)**

becomes

> If your budget is tight and you're interested only in core sauna benefits, then choose a model
> without chromotherapy to save money — **Evidence: strongest of the three options here, and still
> mostly observational (Laukkanen et al., 2015; Hussain & Cohen, 2018)**

**CITED FOR THE UNKNOWN.** Three sentences give a dose sourced to a review whose conclusion asks
for study "to determine the optimal frequency and duration". All three defer instead — see D.

## D. Frequency defers to the published article

Three sentences restate a dose. All three point at the page that answers it properly:

1. "Session context: Infrared sauna general-use guidance often suggests 20–30 minute sessions, one to four times per week, aligning your color exposure with standard sauna safety limits (Laukkanen et al., 2018)."
   → **"Session context: your colour exposure is bounded by the session itself, so the frequency and duration question is the sauna's, not the lighting's. The evidence on how often to use a sauna is covered in [how often should you use a sauna](/blogs/saunas/how-often-should-you-use-sauna)."**
2. "Many wellness guides suggest 20–30 minute sessions, one to four times weekly for infrared saunas (Laukkanen et al., 2018)."
   → **"Session length and frequency follow standard sauna guidance rather than anything specific to the lighting. See [how often should you use a sauna](/blogs/saunas/how-often-should-you-use-sauna) for what the cohort evidence supports."**
3. "Most studies and wellness guides suggest moderate session lengths (typically 15–30 minutes) to avoid overheating (Laukkanen et al., 2018)."
   → **"Session length should follow ordinary sauna guidance, which is set by heat rather than by lighting. See [how often should you use a sauna](/blogs/saunas/how-often-should-you-use-sauna)."**

## F. Standards named, not sourced to lighting vendors

- from: `Sauna manufacturers and third-party lighting suppliers emphasize the importance of ETL, UL, CSA, or IEC 60335-2-53 compliance for fixtures exposed to heat and moisture (Radiant Health Saunas, PDF; China Beauty Lighting, 2025).`
- to: `IEC 60335-2-53 covers electrical safety requirements for sauna heating appliances, and ETL, UL and CSA listings show a product has been tested against the applicable North American standards. Check the current edition and confirm the listing covers the lighting as installed.`

- from: `Some RLT safety standards like IEC 62471 (photobiological safety of lamps) and RoHS (restriction of hazardous substances) also apply to well-designed light therapy devices, highlighting the value of comprehensive testing (RedDot LED, 2025).`
- to: `IEC 62471 governs photobiological safety of lamps and lamp systems, and RoHS restricts certain hazardous substances. Both apply to light therapy devices; check the current edition of each.`

A vendor is not an authority on a standard. Same rule as NEC Article 680: name the standard, say
what it governs, send the reader to the current edition.

## What this pass DOES NOT TOUCH — the group G guard

**Correct citations of the same paper elsewhere stay exactly as they are.** Four articles name
Hussain & Cohen properly — `sisu-sauna-review`, `what-is-a-german-sauna`,
`downdraft-sauna-ventilation-design-patterns`, `dynamic-saunas-monaco-dyn-6996-01-elite` — and

**None of those is edited.** A pass that normalised every citation of PMC5941775 would rewrite
correct work into a house style nobody asked for, and would bury the four real defects inside
twenty cosmetic ones.

Within chromotherapy itself, also untouched: every chromotherapy claim, the decision framework,
