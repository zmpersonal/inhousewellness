## Sentence-level rewrites

**1. Opening line.**

> **Now:** Saunas improve circulation primarily through heat-driven vasodilation — the widening of blood vessels, especially in the skin — which increases blood flow and lowers peripheral resistance.

> **Proposed:** A traditional sauna holds an air temperature of 150 to 195°F, and most sessions run 15 to 20 minutes. What sustained heat of that kind does to blood vessels and blood flow has been studied for decades, and the findings are consistent in direction and limited in strength. We have collected the research at <a href="https://healthresearchdatabase.com">healthresearchdatabase.com</a> rather than summarising it here, because a sauna retailer is not the right source for what heat does to a body.

**2. Second lead sentence.**

> **Now:** Heat exposure triggers blood vessel dilation, redistributes blood flow throughout your body, and raises your heart rate to levels comparable to brisk walking — all without taking a step.

> **Proposed:** Researchers describe heat exposure as producing vasodilation and a raised heart rate, and several studies compare the effect to light activity. That comparison is theirs, not ours, and it comes with the population and the session length attached. Follow the citation before you rely on it.

**3. Sympathetic nervous system sentence.**

> **Now:** During heat exposure, sympathetic nervous system activity increases, triggering the release of stress hormones like catecholamines.

> **Proposed:** Cut. This is a physiological mechanism with no product fact attached, and the evidence site is the right home for it.

**4. Section heading.**

> **Now:** Chronic: The Two Ways Saunas Boost Blood Flow

> **Proposed:** What the Studies Measured, and Over What Period

**5. Cohort figure.**

> **Now:** Compared with men who used saunas once weekly, those bathing 2–3 times per week had a 22% lower risk of sudden cardiac death…

> **Proposed:** Keep the figure, keep the citation, and add the limitation the source states: a Finnish observational cohort of middle-aged men, which cannot establish cause and does not transfer automatically to other populations.
