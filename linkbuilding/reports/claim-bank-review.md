## Read this first — the 7 `preliminary` claims

These rest on mechanistic, small or single-study evidence. They are
grouped here so you can **strike the whole tier in one decision** if you
would rather not have early-stage findings attributed to you at all.

- **`sleep-slowwave-01`** — Passive body heating in the evening increased slow-wave sleep in a small study of older women with insomnia.
  - *Hedge:* Nine participants, one small crossover study from 1996. This is early and narrow evidence, not an established effect.
  - *Evidence:* RCT
- **`heat-shock-01`** — Repeated passive heat exposure raises intracellular heat shock protein 70, one of the cellular stress responses also triggered by exercise.
  - *Hedge:* This is a cellular marker, not a health outcome. Nobody has shown that raising HSP70 by heating produces a clinical benefit in humans.
  - *Evidence:* meta-analysis
- **`heat-shock-02`** — A single session of passive heating produces a measurable inflammatory and heat shock protein response broadly similar in direction to moderate exercise.
  - *Hedge:* Small mechanistic study measuring blood markers over hours. Similar direction is not equivalent magnitude, and no outcome was measured.
  - *Evidence:* mechanistic
- **`cold-glucose-01`** — In a study of men, cold-induced brown fat activity was accompanied by changes in plasma fatty acids and measures of glucose metabolism.
  - *Hedge:* Small, male-only, short-duration, and measuring biochemical markers rather than diabetes risk or any clinical endpoint.
  - *Evidence:* mechanistic
- **`cold-affect-01`** — A short cold-water immersion increased positive affect in a small imaging study, alongside changes in connectivity between brain networks.
  - *Hedge:* A small first-of-its-kind imaging study. Brain-network changes are not evidence of therapeutic benefit.
  - *Evidence:* mechanistic
- **`heat-mood-01`** — A single session of whole-body hyperthermia reduced depressive symptoms compared with a sham procedure in a randomised trial of adults with major depressive disorder.
  - *Hedge:* One small trial, in a clinical population, using medically supervised whole-body hyperthermia under controlled conditions — not a domestic sauna, and not repeated dosing.
  - *Evidence:* RCT
- **`safety-pregnancy-02`** — One review of uncomplicated pregnancy concluded sauna bathing did not affect fertility and was tolerated by healthy women, which sits alongside — not above — the hyperthermia evidence.
  - *Hedge:* These two literatures genuinely disagree in emphasis. The conservative reading governs: core temperature rise in the first trimester is the risk, and this is a decision for the treating clinician.
  - *Evidence:* review

## Heat and cardiovascular outcomes

### `heat-cv-mortality-01` · strong · prospective cohort

> In a long-running Finnish study of middle-aged men, those who used a sauna four to seven times a week had a lower rate of fatal cardiovascular events than those who went once a week.

**Hedge that must travel with it.** This is an observational association in a population where sauna use is lifelong and near-universal. It does not establish that sauna use caused the difference, and people who sauna often may differ in other ways.

## What this document cannot tell you

**Sample size is missing for most citations, and journal name for all of
them.** This is a limitation of the source, not an oversight.

The research index returns title, authors, identifiers, dates and abstract.
It returns no journal field, and full text was unavailable for every paper
in this bank, so sample sizes could not be read out of article bodies. The
12 citations showing a count are those where the figure appears verbatim in
the retrieved abstract. The rest read *sample size not stated* — that means
not stated, not zero, and it was not inferred, summed from subgroups, or
recalled from memory.

Journal name was deliberately dropped rather than derived from the DOI
prefix: prefix `10.1001` covers JAMA, JAMA Internal Medicine and JAMA
Cardiology alike, so deriving it risks naming a journal a paper was never
published in. Every DOI below resolves to the paper of record — please use
it where the venue matters to your judgement.
